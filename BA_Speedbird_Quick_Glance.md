## Company Snapshot

| | |
|---|---|

| **HQ** | Waterside, Harmondsworth, near Heathrow — where your assessment is held |

| **IATA / ICAO / Callsign** | BA · BAW · **Speedbird** — the academy is named after the callsign |

| **Scale (FY2025)** | Operating profit **£2,230m** (up £182m on 2024) · **15.2% operating margin** · ~46.7m passengers · 183 destinations |
| **Brand** | "A British Original" (2022 rebrand) — original thinking, British character, premium service |

## Transformation & Sustainability

- **£7bn transformation programme** (announced 2024): new cabins and First suite, IT modernisation, operational resilience, and customer experience investment.
- **BA Better World**: net zero carbon by **2050**; **10% Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) by 2030**; investments in SAF production partnerships and fleet renewal (each new-generation aircraft burns 20–30% less fuel per seat).

## Speedbird Pilot Academy — Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Launched** | 2023 — BA's first ever **fully funded** cadet scheme (covers the ~£100k+ training cost) |
| **2026 intake** | **160 fully funded places · £18m investment** — the biggest intake yet |
| **Training partners** | **Skyborne Airline Academy** (UK preferred supplier — Gloucestershire ground school, Vero Beach Florida flying) and FTEJerez (Spain) |
| **Your pathway** | Skyborne: ATPL ground school in Gloucestershire → single-engine CPL + UPRT at Vero Beach, FL → multi-engine IR + APS MCC back in the UK → A320 type rating → BA short-haul |
| **Selection** | Stage 1: online application + aptitude testing → **Stage 2: flight-school (Skyborne) online testing + in-person assessment day + online interview** → Stage 3: BA final assessment centre at Waterside |
| **Why it matters to BA** | Removes the financial barrier to entry, widens the talent pool, and secures a pilot pipeline as BA grows and a generation of pilots retires |

## Talking Points That Show Depth

1. **IAG structure** — BA sits alongside Iberia, Aer Lingus, Vueling and LEVEL under IAG. IAG's 2025 results were strong across the group; BA's £2.23bn operating profit led it.
2. **Fleet strategy** — retiring older 777s over time, 787-10s arriving 2027–2030, A350-1000s and refitted A380s carrying the flagship routes. New cadets start on the A320 family short-haul.
3. **Premium strategy** — BA's margin comes disproportionately from premium cabins and transatlantic routes; the £7bn programme is about defending that position.
4. **Speedbird heritage** — the callsign dates back to the 1930s Imperial Airways logo. Naming the academy after it deliberately ties cadets to a century of British aviation.
5. **Why fully funded matters** — say it in your own words: BA is investing in people who could never have self-funded, which builds loyalty and diversity of background on the flight deck. You are exactly that case.
